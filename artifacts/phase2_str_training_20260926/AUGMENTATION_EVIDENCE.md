# Augmentation literature and task-specific screening (Phase-2 development only)

Primary sources checked 2026-09-26:

1. Um et al., *Data Augmentation of Wearable Sensor Data for Parkinson's Disease Monitoring using CNNs* (ICMI 2017), https://arxiv.org/html/1706.00527v2 . Their wrist accelerometry task is PD motor-state classification, not the present PD-versus-differential-diagnosis subject task. They explicitly note that acceleration scaling can alter movement-intensity labels; jitter and heavy cropping hurt in their experiment, while some rotation/temporal transformations helped. No numeric gain transfers to PADS.
2. *Classification of mild Parkinson's disease: data augmentation of time-series gait data obtained via inertial measurement units* (2023), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10400620/ . This is shank gait PD-versus-healthy, a different body site and label, but it evaluates single IMU transformations (noise, scaling, warps, cropping, rotation) and emphasizes that applicability differs by signal. Its successes do not justify aggressive PADS wrist transforms.
3. *Deep neural networks for wearable sensor-based activity recognition in Parkinson's disease: investigating generalizability and model complexity* (2024), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858599/ . Orientation/noise simulation is physically motivated when it represents sensor placement; that does not license arbitrary 3-D rotation of already aligned, bilateral, activity-specific PADS signals.
4. *A comprehensive survey and comparative analysis of time series data augmentation in medical wearable computing* (2025), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957733/ . Time-series label preservation and class imbalance are explicit constraints, and augmentation effects are dataset-dependent. The original Um paper is the preferred source for its own experiment.

PADS-specific decision before tuning: use mild train-only jitter as a possible sensor-noise tolerance test; mild global amplitude scaling as a limited placement/calibration tolerance test; endpoint-preserving temporal perturbation with max 1% displacement; and conservative same-class mixup only if the resulting signals remain physically interpretable. Check actual tensor semantics and limit perturbations to valid activity length and valid wrist data. No validation augmentation. These are supervised training exposures of existing subjects, never new independent subjects. Compare equal PD/DD perturbation with DD-focused perturbation without changing the split or normalization. Because phenotype can depend on amplitude and rhythm, no large scaling, abrupt permutation/reversal, aggressive crop, unrestricted rotation, GAN/diffusion or raw SMOTE. Any candidate that raises Accuracy while BA/F1/DD recall falls is a negative result.
